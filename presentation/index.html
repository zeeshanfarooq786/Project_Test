Pakistan Policy Briefing
01 — The argument

Pakistan does not have a revenue problem first.
It has a documentation problem.

A documented economy means sales, salaries, property, imports and bank flows are visible to the state — so tax can be collected fairly, credit can be given honestly, and policy can be based on facts rather than guesses.

  • The salaried class and a thin layer of companies already pay.
  • A large share of retail, real estate, agriculture wealth and cash trade does not.
  • Governments then raise rates on the visible few, which pushes more activity underground.

This briefing answers four questions

  1. How can Pakistan actually achieve documentation?
  2. What are the benefits if we do?
  3. What punishment applies if a person does not comply — and if the law is enforced?
  4. What problems are we already facing by not doing this?

The extra “Pakistan complete picture” slide later in this deck puts all four answers on one page.

03 — Definition

What “doing this” actually means

01 · Citizen

File, declare, stay on the ATL

Every person with taxable income files an income-tax return on time, stays on the Active Taxpayer List, and can explain large purchases from declared wealth.

02 · Business

Books, invoices, banked sales

Shops and firms issue invoices, keep purchase and sales records, use bank or digital payment where required, and — if notified — connect POS / e-invoicing to FBR.

03 · State

Simple rules, equal enforcement

The state makes compliance cheaper than hiding, stops harassing honest filers, and uses data (utilities, property, imports, banking) instead of raids as the default tool.

Documentation is not “more tax on the same people.” It is bringing hidden turnover into the light so rates on the documented minority can eventually come down.

04 — How we achieve this

How Pakistan can achieve a documented economy

01

Make filing easy

IRIS, mobile apps, Urdu/regional forms, tax facilitation centres. The 2026 Fixed Tax Asaan Scheme (1% of turnover, minimum Rs 25,000, sales up to Rs 200m) is built for this.

02

Reward the filer

Lowest withholding rates, refunds, bank accounts, property and vehicle access, government contracts and cheaper credit must belong to people on the ATL.

03

See the transaction

POS and e-invoicing for large retail, restaurants and notified sectors; NADRA / land / customs / bank data matched to returns so lifestyle and income cannot stay strangers.

04

Protect the honest

No random shop raids on scheme participants, association-based dispute resolution, and a real end to “audit as harassment.” Fear is why Tajir Dost failed.

05

Enforce the big leaks

Apply penalties, Section 114C high-value blocks, and sealing powers on persistent non-filers and fake invoicing — not on the smallest compliant trader.

Path: simplify → reward → digitise → protect → punish the leak, not the compliant

05 — Benefits

Benefits if Pakistan actually does this

For the country

  • More revenue without crushing the same 2.5 million people — a wider base funds schools, hospitals, water and defence without a new IMF squeeze every cycle.
  • Lower fiscal deficit and less debt interest — FY2026 already showed what a primary surplus can do; a broader base makes it lasting.
  • Cheaper electricity and fewer circular-debt patches because subsidies can be targeted, not sprayed.
  • Better policy — you cannot plan industry, exports or cities if half of GDP is a rumour.

For the citizen and the honest business

  • Filer status / ATL: lowest withholding on property, cars, bank profit, dividends; refund of extra tax; ability to open normal bank accounts.
  • Credit: documented sales are the passport to bank loans, export certification and government tenders.
  • Fair competition: the shop that issues invoices stops losing to the shop that hides 40% of turnover.
  • Dignity: a return is proof of contribution. It is also proof of income when you need a visa, a plot, or a court.

06 — Complete Pakistan picture

How we achieve this in Pakistan — benefits, punishments, and the cost of not doing it

One slide. Four answers. This is the civic choice in front of the country.

A · How we achieve it

  • Every earner files; traders join Asaan Tax (1% turnover, min Rs 25,000) or the regular return.
  • Large retail / notified sectors live on FBR e-invoices and POS — no sale off-system.
  • Banks, land records, NADRA, customs and utilities talk to IRIS so lifestyle matches income.
  • The state cuts harassment, keeps rates predictable, and spends visibly so people see a return on tax.
  • Provinces document urban property and agricultural wealth instead of leaving the federal salaried class alone in the net.

B · Benefits if we do it

  • Wider tax base → room to lower rates on those already paying.
  • ATL benefits: cheaper withholding, refunds, cars, plots, bank accounts, tenders.
  • Banks can lend against real books; investment and jobs follow documentation.
  • State can fund education, health, water and climate without repeating debt crises.
  • Informal 35–40% of GDP slowly becomes countable, taxable, and financeable.

C · Punishment if one does not apply / if the law is applied

  • Non-filer: 2–4× withholding on banks, property, cars, dividends; no ordinary new bank account; no refund.
  • Late filer: daily default penalty + ATL surcharge (individuals Rs 25,000 in 2026-27) to get back on the list.
  • Concealment / false particulars: penalty raised to Rs 1 million (Finance Bill 2026) plus tax evaded.
  • No records on audit: Rs 100,000 / 200,000 / 300,000 by notice. Failure to withhold: Rs 500,000; company officers personally Rs 500,000.
  • Section 114C (in law; gazette notification still pending): blocks cars over Rs 7m, homes over Rs 50m, commercial property over Rs 100m, securities over Rs 50m/year, cash out over Rs 100m/year unless resources are declared at 130% of the deal.
  • Persistent default: higher fines and power to seal business premises.

D · Problems we face by not doing this

  • The documented minority is over-taxed; the undocumented majority is invisible — a justice failure, not only a fiscal one.
  • Every budget becomes an IMF negotiation because the base is too small for the state’s bills.
  • Cash, smuggling and benami property hide crime, terror finance and elite tax evasion in the same fog.
  • Honest businesses cannot compete; talent leaves; investment stays short-term and speculative.
  • No data means bad schools, leaking power, unplanned cities, and subsidies that never reach the poor they were meant for.

07 — Punishment if applied

What the law does to you if you do not apply

If you… Status What hits you
File on time and stay truthful Active filer (ATL) Lowest withholding; refunds allowed; full economic access; still must keep books.
File after the deadline Late filer Daily penalty (Finance Bill 2026 proposed a sharp increase, including Rs 2,000/day in some sales-tax late cases). ATL surcharge to return to the list (Rs 25,000 for individuals, 2026-27).
Never file Non-filer 2–4× withholding on almost every documented transaction. New bank accounts largely blocked. No refund. Highest friction on property and vehicles.
Hide income or file false details Evader Penalty up to Rs 1,000,000 plus the tax evaded. Repeat behaviour can go higher. Criminal provisions remain available for tax fraud.
Ignore audit notices / don’t withhold Defaulter Record penalties Rs 100k–300k; withholding failure Rs 500,000; company principals personally Rs 500,000. Premises can be sealed after continued default.
Try a large lifestyle with no declared wealth Ineligible person (s.114C) Once notified: cannot book a car above Rs 7m, a house above Rs 50m FMV, commercial property above Rs 100m, heavy securities, or pull Rs 100m+ cash in a year without 130% declared resources.

Note: Section 114C is already in the Income Tax Ordinance (Finance Act 2025) but, as of August 2026 reporting, the federal gazette date and operational rules were still pending. Withholding and ATL disadvantages already apply.

08 — Cost of not doing this

Problems we are already facing by not doing this

01

A state that cannot pay for itself

Interest on old debt eats the budget. Development is residual. We borrow to run the government because too few people are on the books. That is why every “record collection” still feels like a crisis.

02

The salaried class as a hostage

Tax is deducted from the salary slip before the employee sees it. Retail, wholesale, real-estate flipping and cash professions often pay nothing comparable. People learn that honesty is expensive.

03

Fake competition

A documented factory paying payroll, GST and power at industrial rates cannot beat a cash competitor. Industry thins out. Imports and trading fill the gap. Jobs that should exist never appear.

04

Black money has a home

Undocumented plots, prize bonds, cash and under-invoiced imports are where corruption, smuggling and tax evasion hide. Without trails, courts and banks are guessing.

05

No credit, no scale

Banks will not lend against a notebook in a drawer. So firms stay small, families stay in the informal loop, and the country stays a trading economy instead of a producing one.

06

Trust collapse

Citizens say: “Why should I pay if the elite does not, and if my tax disappears?” The state says: “Why should I improve service if nobody pays?” Both are waiting. Both are wrong to wait.

Not doing documentation is not neutral. It is a daily policy choice with victims.

09 — Fairness

Who pays today — and who should

Over-documented

Salaried employees, large companies, importers at the port, formal banks

  • Cannot hide: withholding is automatic.
  • Face audits, payroll tax, sales tax, WWF/WPPF, and rising rates whenever the deficit yawns.
  • This is why “tax culture” speeches sound empty to a teacher or a software employee.

Under-documented

Cash retail, parts of wholesale, speculators, some professional cash practices, much real-estate gain

  • About 4.2–4.4 million traders are the Asaan Tax target — most still outside a serious return.
  • High-value cars and houses are often bought without income that matches the price.
  • Agriculture income is provincial; urban agri-wealth and lifestyle often escape both federation and province.

A just policy does not hunt the easy target. It documents the large leak, simplifies the small trader, and then lowers rates for everyone who stayed honest.

10 — Practical path

A 12-month path that can actually work

Citizens

File this year. Stay on ATL.

  • Create an IRIS account / FBR app login.
  • File even if income is modest — filer status is an asset.
  • Keep bank trails for rent, fees, and large buys.
  • Do not “save” tax by staying a non-filer: the extra withholding is usually larger than the tax due.

Traders & SMEs

Pick the simple door, then grow.

  • If turnover ≤ Rs 200m: Fixed Tax Asaan Scheme — 1% of sales, minimum Rs 25,000, no routine POS/audit.
  • Keep a basic sales and purchase book anyway. Under-declaring now is a future raid.
  • If you scale past the scheme, integrate e-invoicing before FBR forces it.

The state

Stop repeating Tajir Dost.

  • Honour the no-harassment promise on scheme shops.
  • Notify Section 114C with clear rules — then apply it to luxury leaks, not kiryana stores.
  • Publish where the extra rupee goes: schools, water, local hospitals.
  • Match property, cars and electricity with returns. Quiet data beats noisy raids.

If we do this

A country that can see itself

  • Tax base expands; rates on the salaried can be discussed downward in good faith.
  • IMF programmes become shorter and less humiliating because the numbers add up.
  • Banks finance factories, not only plots.
  • Luxury that cannot be explained is blocked at the registration desk.
  • A teacher and a trader stand in the same civic line: both documented, both served.

If we do not

The same cycle, more expensive

  • Another budget of higher penalties on the same trapped base.
  • Capital and talent leave; remaining businesses stay informal on purpose.
  • Debt interest remains the largest “development” line.
  • Smog, water, schools and hospitals stay underfunded while cash houses rise in DHA.
  • The next generation inherits a state that still cannot count its own economy.
